# A Day of One-Line Pitches

A single-sentence pitch that would convince a friend who has never coded to let an AI agent build their first app idea on make.fun. The entry is one plain, warm sentence that names make.fun, names what the agent does with the idea, and makes handing it over feel easy and low risk.

> Tell make.fun your app idea in everyday words and the AI agent hands you back a working first version you can open and try tonight.

## Who this pitch speaks to

This sentence is aimed at a friend who has never written a line of code but has an idea they keep meaning to try. It meets them where they are: no jargon, no setup, no promise that they must learn anything first. The word "everyday" is the handshake — it says your idea is enough, exactly as you can say it out loud.

## Why each phrase earns its place

"Tell make.fun" opens with a direct, friendly command so the friend knows the very first step. "your app idea in everyday words" lowers the barrier to zero — no technical vocabulary required. "the AI agent hands you back" makes the result feel like a gift placed in their hands rather than a task they must finish. "a working first version" is honest: it is a first version, not a perfect app, so nothing is overpromised. "you can open and try tonight" gives a concrete, immediate reason to say yes now.

## How it works

`main.py` uses only the Python standard library. It is fully deterministic: it holds the winning sentence as a string literal, self-checks that the sentence is non-empty, not whitespace-only, and at most 240 characters, then writes it (UTF-8, no trailing newline) to `/output/entry.txt`.
